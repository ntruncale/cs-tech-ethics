# Teacher Film Guide: *Fantastic Four: First Steps*

This guide is for the class viewing that follows Modules 1–6. Students complete **Module 7** around the viewing: Steps 1–2 before, Steps 3–6 after. This file contains no answers, so it can stay in the public repository.

> **Preview the film first.** The pause points below are described by story beat, not timestamp, because times vary between copies and streaming versions. Adjust them to match what you see.

---

## About the film

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | *The Fantastic Four: First Steps* (Marvel Studios, 2025) |
| **Director** | Matt Shakman |
| **Rating** | PG-13 |
| **Length** | About 2 hours (check your copy) |
| **Setting** | A retro-futuristic, 1960s-style world ("Earth-828") with robots and flying cars, but no internet, smartphones or social media as we know them |

**Why this film:** The Fantastic Four are the most famous family on their planet, and the story centers on the birth of Reed and Sue's son, Franklin. A threat to the whole world turns that private family event into a public question. That makes it a good fit for every lens in the course: footprint, data collection, protection, bias, society and rights.

## Before you show it

- [ ] **School policy on PG-13 films.** If you need family permission, send [`family-letter.md`](family-letter.md) a week ahead and plan an alternative activity for any student who opts out (for example, Module 7's Context and Learn steps plus a privacy analysis of a G- or PG-rated film or a news story).
- [ ] **Screening rights.** U.S. copyright law (17 U.S.C. § 110(1)) generally allows a lawfully made copy of a film to be shown in face-to-face teaching at a nonprofit school when it's part of instruction, not entertainment. Streaming services' terms of use can be stricter. Check with your librarian or administration about the copy you'll use.
- [ ] **Content heads-up.** Superhero action and peril, some language, and themes of pregnancy, childbirth and a threat to a baby. Preview so you can prepare students who may find those themes difficult.
- [ ] **Print** [`viewing-guide.md`](viewing-guide.md) for each student (or have them take notes on paper).
- [ ] Students read **Module 7, Steps 1–2** (about 10 minutes) before Day 1.

## Viewing plan

| Schedule | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Three 45–50 minute periods** | Day 1: start the film (about 40 min) + 5 min of notes. Day 2: continue (about 45 min). Day 3: finish (about 35 min) + 10 min discussion; Module 7 Steps 3–6 for homework or the next class. |
| **Two block periods** | Block 1: about 75 min of film + 10 min of notes. Block 2: finish (about 45 min) + 30 min discussion or Socratic seminar. Module 7 Steps 3–6 to finish. |

Keep the pauses short (2–3 minutes). Students jot a note under one lens on the viewing guide, then you continue. Save longer discussion for the end.

## Pause points by lens

These are approximate story beats. Confirm them while previewing.

| # | Pause after… | Lens | Quick prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The opening introduction to the family and how the world sees them | 👣 Footprint | What does the world know about this family? Who created that information? |
| 2 | Early scenes of the family at home with H.E.R.B.I.E. and Reed's lab | 📡 Collection | Who collects information about whom in this house? Would you want a helper like H.E.R.B.I.E. at home? |
| 3 | The team learns what Galactus wants | 🏛️ Society | Should the family tell the world? Who has a right to know? |
| 4 | Franklin's birth and the family's return home | 🛡️ Rights · 🔒 Protection | Franklin can't consent to anything yet. Who is protecting his privacy and safety, and how? |
| 5 | The public's reaction once the truth is out | ⚖️ Bias | How are people judging the family? What information do they have, and what are they missing? |
| 6 | The family speaks to the public | 🏛️ Society · 🛡️ Rights | What does the family choose to share, and why? Does it change people's minds? |
| 7 | The end of the film | All six | Which lens matters most to how the story turned out? |

## Discussion questions

Use a few after the film, or as seminar questions.

**👣 Footprint (M1)**
- Most of the family's "footprint" was created by reporters, TV hosts and fans. How is that different from a footprint you create yourself?
- What would the family have wanted to keep private? Could they?

**📡 Collection (M2)**
- Reed is a scientist who studies everything, including his own family. When is collecting information about someone an act of care, and when does it cross a line?
- H.E.R.B.I.E. sees and hears everything at home. What rules would you write for a real home robot?

**🔒 Protection (M3)**
- What does the family do to protect Franklin? What does it cost them?
- What would protecting Franklin's information look like in our world: passwords, encryption, privacy settings?

**⚖️ Bias (M4)**
- How did people judge the family with only part of the story? Where did their information come from?
- How is a crowd reacting to incomplete information like an algorithm trained on incomplete data?

**🏛️ Society (M5)**
- Did the world have a right to know what the family knew? Where's the line between the public's right to know and a family's privacy?
- Should a decision that affects the whole planet be made by one family, by governments, or by everyone? How?

**🛡️ Rights (M6)**
- Franklin is a newborn. Who should speak for his privacy?
- Imagine Franklin at 16, looking back at how the world talked about him as a baby. What would he want deleted, if he could? (Connect to the right to be forgotten.)

**Their world and ours**
- The film's world has no smartphones or social media. Pick one moment and describe how it would play out in 2026: what would be filmed, posted, tracked, sold or searched?

## Optional: Socratic seminar (one period)

**Central question:** *When the whole world wants to know, who decides what stays private?*

1. **Setup (5 min).** Inner circle of 8–10 students, outer circle observing. Students bring their viewing guides and Module 7 notes.
2. **Inner circle (15 min).** Students discuss the central question, citing a moment from the film and an idea from at least one module. You only step in to redirect or to ask "Can you point to a moment?"
3. **Outer circle feedback (5 min).** Each observer shares one strong point they heard and one question nobody asked.
4. **Switch circles (15 min).**
5. **Exit ticket (5 min).** *Did your answer to the central question change? Why or why not?*

**Quick participation check (per student):**

| | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evidence** | No reference to the film or the course | Refers to the film or the course | Cites a specific moment and a course idea |
| **Reasoning** | Opinion only | Explains their reasoning | Weighs both sides of the trade-off |
| **Listening** | Talks past others | Responds to others | Builds on or respectfully challenges others by name |

## Optional extension: "Earth-828 goes online"

Students rewrite one scene from the film as if it happened in our world in 2026: include at least three kinds of data that would be created (posts, location, metadata, facial recognition, data brokers…), who would collect each, and what one law or right from Module 5 or 6 would protect the family. Formats: a script, a comic strip, a news feed mock-up or a short essay.
